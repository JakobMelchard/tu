# B06 Quantum complexity: BQP, QMA and query complexity

This note places quantum computation into the classical landscape of B01 to B05. The model is a uniform family of quantum circuits; the class BQP is defined like BPP with quantum circuits; everything known about its position ($\mathrm{BPP} \subseteq \mathrm{BQP} \subseteq \mathrm{PP} \subseteq \mathrm{PSPACE}$) is proved by two ideas, reversible simulation of classical circuits and the Feynman path sum. QMA is the quantum NP, with k-LOCAL HAMILTONIAN as its Cook-Levin problem. Query complexity is the one setting where quantum speedups are *provable*: Grover's $\sqrt n$ is optimal, parity gains only a factor 2, and the polynomial method turns algorithms into low-degree polynomials. References: de Wolf ch. 13-14 [S31] and Watrous [S32] for BQP and QMA; Bernstein-Vazirani for the definition, robustness and BQP subset PSPACE [S37]; Adleman-DeMarrais-Huang for BQP subset PP [S54]; Kitaev-Shen-Vyalyi and Kempe-Kitaev-Regev for QMA and k-LOCAL HAMILTONIAN [S55]; Raz-Tal for the oracle against BQP subset PH [S53]; BBBV for the search lower bound [S41]; Beals et al. and Ambainis for the polynomial and adversary methods [S57]; Nielsen & Chuang 3.2, 4.5-4.6, 6.6-6.7 [S30]; Aaronson ch. 10, 13 [S27]; Complexity Zoo for the inclusion diagram [S34]. TISS files BQP, QMA, quantum query complexity and the extended Church-Turing thesis under *Complexity theory* [S1], but they are examined on **Exam 2** (21 Jan), whose title is "Quantum Algorithms and Complexity".

## Definitions

- **Quantum circuit** on $m$ qubits over a finite **gate set** $G$ (e.g. $\{H, T, \mathrm{CNOT}\}$, or $\{H, \mathrm{Toffoli}\}$, both universal): a sequence $U_1, \dots, U_\ell$ of gates from $G$ applied to specified qubits; size $\ell$, depth as for classical circuits. Input $x \in \{0,1\}^n$ is loaded as $|x\rangle |0^{m-n}\rangle$; the output is the measurement of qubit 1 in the computational basis. Intermediate measurements can be deferred to the end (principle of deferred measurement), so this is WLOG.
- **Uniform family** $\{C_n\}$: a classical TM computes $1^n \mapsto \langle C_n \rangle$ in polynomial time (or log space; equivalent for BQP, B04). Uniformity is what makes "a circuit exists" an algorithmic statement.
- $\mathbf{BQP}$: $L \in \mathrm{BQP}$ iff there is a uniform polynomial-size family with $\Pr[C_n(x) = [x \in L]] \ge 2/3$ for all $x$. $\mathrm{EQP}$: error 0. $\mathrm{BQP}$ is the quantum analogue of BPP, not of P.
- **Extended Church-Turing thesis (ECT).** Every physically realisable model of computation is simulable by a probabilistic TM with polynomial overhead. The plain Church-Turing thesis (computability) is untouched by quantum mechanics: BQP problems are decidable. The ECT is challenged by Shor: FACTORING $\in \mathrm{BQP}$, and no polynomial classical algorithm is known (best: number field sieve, $\exp(O(n^{1/3} \log^{2/3} n))$). Either the ECT is false, or factoring is classically easy, or scalable quantum computers cannot be built; the last is a physics claim, not a complexity one.
- $\mathbf{MA}$: NP with a randomised verifier (Merlin sends a classical witness $w$, Arthur runs a BPP check; completeness $2/3$, soundness $1/3$). $\mathbf{QCMA}$: classical witness, quantum verifier. $\mathbf{QMA}$: quantum verifier $V$ (uniform circuit family) and quantum witness $|\psi\rangle$ on $p(n)$ qubits: $x \in L \Rightarrow \exists |\psi\rangle : \Pr[V(x, |\psi\rangle) = 1] \ge 2/3$; $x \notin L \Rightarrow \forall |\psi\rangle : \Pr[V(x, |\psi\rangle) = 1] \le 1/3$. $\mathrm{NP} \subseteq \mathrm{MA} \subseteq \mathrm{QCMA} \subseteq \mathrm{QMA}$, $\mathrm{BQP} \subseteq \mathrm{QMA}$ (ignore the witness).
- **$k$-LOCAL HAMILTONIAN ($k$-LH).** Input: $H = \sum_{j=1}^{r} H_j$ on $n$ qubits, each $H_j$ Hermitian, acting on $\le k$ qubits, $0 \le H_j \le I$, entries given to polynomial precision; thresholds $a < b$ with $b - a \ge 1/\mathrm{poly}(n)$; promise that $\lambda_{\min}(H) \le a$ or $\ge b$. Decide which. This is the quantum analogue of MAX-$k$-SAT (each $H_j$ is a "soft clause" penalising some local configurations).
- **Query model.** Input $x \in \{0,1\}^n$ accessible only through an oracle. Standard (bit-flip) oracle $O_x |i, b\rangle = |i, b \oplus x_i\rangle$; **phase oracle** $O_x |i\rangle = (-1)^{x_i} |i\rangle$, obtained from the standard one by setting $b = |-\rangle$ (phase kickback), and conversely one standard query is simulated by one phase query on $|i\rangle|b\rangle$ with a controlled phase. A $t$-query algorithm is $U_t O_x U_{t-1} \cdots O_x U_0 |0\rangle$ followed by a measurement; $U_i$ are arbitrary (unbounded cost) unitaries on the index register plus workspace. $Q(f)$ = min $t$ with error $\le 1/3$ on every input; $Q_E(f)$ exact; $R(f)$ = randomised decision-tree complexity (bounded error); $D(f)$ = deterministic decision-tree depth. Always $Q(f) \le R(f) \le D(f) \le n$.
- **Degree.** $\deg(f)$: degree of the unique multilinear real polynomial equal to $f$ on $\{0,1\}^n$. **Approximate degree** $\widetilde{\deg}(f)$: min degree of a polynomial $p$ with $|p(x) - f(x)| \le 1/3$ for all $x \in \{0,1\}^n$.

## Results

**Robustness of BQP.**
1. *Threshold.* $2/3$ can be replaced by $1/2 + 1/\mathrm{poly}(n)$ or $1 - 2^{-\mathrm{poly}(n)}$: run $k$ independent copies (fresh ancillas, deferred measurement), output the majority; Chernoff exactly as in B05. This uses that the circuit can be repeated with independent outcomes, which is true for BQP and QMA-verifiers but fails for PP.
2. *Gate set independence (Solovay-Kitaev).* For any two finite universal gate sets $G_1, G_2$ closed under inverses, every gate of $G_1$ (on $O(1)$ qubits) is approximated to operator-norm error $\varepsilon$ by a sequence of $O(\log^c(1/\varepsilon))$ gates from $G_2$, $c \approx 3.97$ in the original proof, $c = 1 + o(1)$ for Clifford+T by number-theoretic synthesis (Ross-Selinger). Errors add: $\|U_\ell \cdots U_1 - V_\ell \cdots V_1\| \le \sum_i \|U_i - V_i\|$ (unitarity, telescoping). A circuit of size $\ell$ with per-gate error $\varepsilon = 1/(10\ell)$ has total error $\le 1/10$, changing acceptance probabilities by $\le 2 \cdot 0.1$; the overhead is $O(\ell \log^c(10 \ell))$, polynomial. (Gate sets with non-algebraic entries could encode uncomputable information; the standard convention restricts to algebraic entries, or simply fixes $\{H, T, \mathrm{CNOT}\}$.)
3. *Measurements, ancillas, mixed states* do not change the class either.

**Theorem.** $\mathrm{BPP} \subseteq \mathrm{BQP}$ (and $\mathrm P \subseteq \mathrm{BQP}$).
*Proof.* A polynomial-time classical computation is a uniform polynomial-size Boolean circuit (B04, tableau construction). Replace each gate by a reversible one: NAND$(a, b)$ is Toffoli on $|a, b, 1\rangle$ (target becomes $1 \oplus ab$), fan-out is CNOT on $|a, 0\rangle$, so the circuit becomes a reversible circuit $R$ with $|x, 0\rangle \mapsto |x, f(x), \text{garbage}\rangle$. Copy $f(x)$ to a clean qubit with a CNOT and run $R^{-1}$ (Bennett's uncompute trick) to clear the garbage: $|x, 0, 0\rangle \mapsto |x, 0, f(x)\rangle$, size $2|R| + 1$, still uniform. Randomness: a fair coin is $H|0\rangle$ measured (or kept coherent, using deferred measurement); a BPP machine with $m$ random bits becomes a quantum circuit on $m$ extra qubits initialised with $H^{\otimes m}$, and its acceptance probability is unchanged. $\square$

**Theorem (Feynman path sum).** $\mathrm{BQP} \subseteq \mathrm{PSPACE}$; more precisely $\mathrm{BQP} \subseteq \mathrm{PP} \subseteq \mathrm P^{\#\mathrm P}$ (Adleman-DeMarrais-Huang 1997 [S54], Bernstein-Vazirani 1997 [S37]).
*Proof.* Let $C = U_\ell \cdots U_1$ on $m = \mathrm{poly}(n)$ qubits, gates from $\{H, \mathrm{Toffoli}\}$ (universal, real entries in $\{0, \pm 1, \pm 1/\sqrt 2\}$). Insert resolutions of the identity between gates:
$$\langle y | C | x, 0\rangle = \sum_{y_1, \dots, y_{\ell - 1} \in \{0,1\}^m} \langle y | U_\ell | y_{\ell-1}\rangle \langle y_{\ell-1} | U_{\ell-1} | y_{\ell-2}\rangle \cdots \langle y_1 | U_1 | x, 0\rangle .$$
Each factor $\langle y_i | U_i | y_{i-1}\rangle$ is a matrix element of a gate on $\le 3$ qubits tensored with identity on the rest: it is $0$ unless $y_i$ and $y_{i-1}$ agree outside those qubits, and otherwise it is a $\pm 2^{-1/2}$ or $\pm 1$ entry, computable in polynomial time. A **path** is a sequence $(y_1, \dots, y_{\ell-1})$; there are $2^{m(\ell - 1)}$, but each is described by $m \ell$ bits. *PSPACE:* enumerate the paths in lexicographic order, multiply the $\ell$ factors, accumulate the sum (a rational number with $O(\ell)$ bits of numerator and a power of $\sqrt 2$ as denominator: exact arithmetic). Then $\Pr[\text{accept}] = \sum_{y : y_1 = 1} |\langle y | C | x, 0\rangle|^2$: another polynomial-space enumeration around the first one. Space $O(m \ell + \text{arithmetic})$, time $2^{O(m\ell)}$. *PP / #P:* after pulling out the common factor $2^{-h/2}$ ($h$ = number of Hadamards), each nonzero path contributes $\pm 1$, so an amplitude is $(N_+ - N_-)\, 2^{-h/2}$ with $N_\pm$ = number of paths of sign $\pm$; $N_\pm$ are #P functions (the NTM guesses the path and checks the sign). The acceptance probability is $\sum_y (N_+^y - N_-^y)^2 2^{-h}$, a difference of two #P functions over a polynomial-size sum, so "$\Pr[\text{accept}] > 1/2$" is decidable in $\mathrm P^{\#\mathrm P}$, and with a little more care (writing the probability as a single #P difference, then simulating the comparison by a PP machine) in PP. $\square$
So $\mathrm{BQP} \subseteq \mathrm{PP} \subseteq \mathrm{PSPACE}$ and by Toda (B05) BQP sits inside the counting world that contains PH; but $\mathrm{BQP}$ vs $\mathrm{PH}$ is unknown in both directions (Raz-Tal 2018 [S53]: an oracle relative to which $\mathrm{BQP} \not\subseteq \mathrm{PH}$).

**BQP and NP.** Unknown in both directions; believed incomparable. Evidence that $\mathrm{NP} \not\subseteq \mathrm{BQP}$: relative to a random oracle, and more concretely by the BBBV theorem below, a quantum algorithm needs $\Omega(\sqrt{2^n})$ queries to find a satisfying assignment of a black-box function, so there is an oracle $A$ with $\mathrm{NP}^A \not\subseteq \mathrm{BQP}^A$ (Bennett, Bernstein, Brassard, Vazirani 1997). Aaronson's summary: quantum computers are not expected to solve NP-complete problems in polynomial time; the generic speedup is Grover's square root, and exponential speedups (Shor, Simon) exploit structure (hidden subgroups, periodicity) that NP-complete problems seem not to have. Evidence that $\mathrm{BQP} \not\subseteq \mathrm{NP}$ (or MA): oracle separations, and FACTORING/discrete log are in $\mathrm{NP} \cap \mathrm{coNP} \cap \mathrm{BQP}$, consistent with BQP being "sideways" from NP.

**QMA amplification (Marriott-Watrous 2005, statement).** The completeness/soundness pair $(2/3, 1/3)$ can be replaced by $(1 - 2^{-k}, 2^{-k})$ with $O(k)$ sequential uses of the verifier on the *same* witness, without extra witness copies and with only $O(1)$ ancilla qubits reused. The naive method (Merlin sends $k$ copies, Arthur takes a majority) also works but needs an argument that an entangled multi-copy witness cannot cheat: the optimal acceptance probability of a product verifier is attained by a product witness, since each copy's acceptance is a POVM with eigenvalues $\le 1/3$ on no-instances. Similarly $(c, s)$ with $c - s \ge 1/\mathrm{poly}$ suffices.

**Theorem (Kitaev 1999; Kempe-Kitaev-Regev 2006).** $k$-LH is QMA-complete for $k \ge 2$ (Kitaev proved $k = 5$).
*Membership.* Witness: the ground state $|\psi\rangle$ (or any state with $\langle\psi| H |\psi\rangle \le a$). Verifier: pick $j \in \{1, \dots, r\}$ uniformly, measure the two-outcome POVM $\{H_j, I - H_j\}$ on $|\psi\rangle$ (each $H_j$ acts on $k = O(1)$ qubits, so its eigendecomposition is computable; implement by phase estimation or by a small unitary plus ancilla), accept on outcome $I - H_j$. Then $\Pr[\text{accept}] = 1 - \frac1r \langle\psi| H |\psi\rangle$, which is $\ge 1 - a/r$ on yes-instances and $\le 1 - b/r$ for every $|\psi\rangle$ on no-instances (the minimum of $\langle\psi|H|\psi\rangle$ is $\lambda_{\min} \ge b$). Gap $(b - a)/r \ge 1/\mathrm{poly}$; amplify.
*Hardness (clock construction).* Let $V = U_T \cdots U_1$ be a QMA verifier acting on witness qubits and ancillas $|0\rangle$. Add a clock register of $T + 1$ states (unary, $T$ qubits, $|t\rangle = |1^t 0^{T - t}\rangle$). The **history state** of a witness $|\xi\rangle$ is
$$|\eta_\xi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{T+1}} \sum_{t = 0}^{T} \big(U_t \cdots U_1 |\xi, 0\rangle\big) \otimes |t\rangle .$$
Hamiltonian $H = H_{\mathrm{in}} + H_{\mathrm{prop}} + H_{\mathrm{out}} + H_{\mathrm{clock}}$: $H_{\mathrm{in}} = \sum_{\text{ancilla } a} |1\rangle\langle 1|_a \otimes |0\rangle\langle 0|_{\mathrm{clock}}$ penalises ancillas not initialised at $t = 0$; $H_{\mathrm{out}} = |0\rangle\langle 0|_{\mathrm{out}} \otimes |T\rangle\langle T|$ penalises rejection at time $T$; $H_{\mathrm{clock}}$ penalises non-unary clock strings ($|01\rangle\langle 01|$ on neighbouring clock qubits); and
$$H_{\mathrm{prop}} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \tfrac12 \Big( I \otimes |t\rangle\langle t| + I \otimes |t-1\rangle\langle t-1| - U_t \otimes |t\rangle\langle t-1| - U_t^\dagger \otimes |t-1\rangle\langle t| \Big)$$
whose zero-energy states are exactly the history states (each term checks that the $t$-th component is $U_t$ applied to the $(t-1)$-th). Locality: each term touches $\le 2$ gate qubits and $\le 3$ clock qubits, $k = 5$. If $x$ is a yes-instance, $|\eta_\xi\rangle$ for the good witness has energy $\le \frac{1}{T+1}\Pr[\text{reject}] \le \frac{1}{3(T+1)} =: a$. If $x$ is a no-instance, a spectral gap analysis (projection lemma / geometric lemma: the ground energy of a sum of two positive operators whose kernels meet at angle $\theta$ is $\ge \Omega(\theta^2)$ times the gap) gives $\lambda_{\min} \ge \Omega(T^{-3}) =: b$, with $b - a \ge 1/\mathrm{poly}(T)$ after amplifying the verifier's soundness. $\square$
Refinements: 2-LH on qubits (KKR, perturbation gadgets), 2-LH on a 2D lattice (Oliveira-Terhal), 1D with $d = 8$ (Aharonov-Gottesman-Irani-Kempe). Other QMA-complete problems: QUANTUM $k$-SAT for $k \ge 3$ (Bravyi), consistency of local density matrices, NON-IDENTITY CHECK. Also $\mathrm{QMA} \subseteq \mathrm{PP}$ (Kitaev-Watrous; Marriott-Watrous), hence $\mathrm{QMA} \subseteq \mathrm{PSPACE}$; whether $\mathrm{QMA} = \mathrm{QCMA}$ or $\mathrm{QMA} = \mathrm{MA}$ is open (oracle separations known).

**Query complexity: the examples.**
- **OR$_n$ (search).** $Q(\mathrm{OR}_n) = \Theta(\sqrt n)$. Upper bound: Grover with $\lfloor \frac{\pi}{4} \sqrt{n} \rfloor$ iterations (each one phase query and one diffusion $2|s\rangle\langle s| - I$), success probability $\sin^2((2r+1)\theta)$, $\theta = \arcsin(1/\sqrt n)$. Lower bound (BBBV hybrid argument): let $|\psi_t\rangle$ be the state after $t$ queries on the all-zero input, $|\psi_t^i\rangle$ on input $e_i$. Define the query magnitude $q_t^i = \| P_i |\psi_t\rangle\|^2$ where $P_i$ projects onto index $i$; $\sum_i q_t^i = 1$ per query, so $\sum_i \sum_{t \le T} \sqrt{q_t^i} \le T \sqrt n$ by Cauchy-Schwarz, and some $i$ has $\sum_t \sqrt{q_t^i} \le T/\sqrt n$. Flipping $x_i$ changes the state after query $t$ by at most $2 \sqrt{q_t^i}$ (the oracle differs only on the $P_i$ component), so by the triangle inequality $\| |\psi_T\rangle - |\psi^i_T\rangle \| \le 2T/\sqrt n$. Distinguishing $0^n$ from $e_i$ with error $1/3$ needs constant distance, so $T = \Omega(\sqrt n)$. $D(\mathrm{OR}_n) = R(\mathrm{OR}_n) = \Theta(n)$ (any classical algorithm must look at $\Omega(n)$ bits to have constant success on $0^n$ vs a random $e_i$).
- **PARITY$_n$.** $Q_E(\mathrm{PARITY}_n) = \lceil n/2 \rceil$: Deutsch's algorithm computes $x_i \oplus x_j$ with one phase query, $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|i\rangle + |j\rangle) \mapsto \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}((-1)^{x_i}|i\rangle + (-1)^{x_j}|j\rangle)$, which is $\pm|+_{ij}\rangle$ if $x_i = x_j$ and $\pm|-_{ij}\rangle$ otherwise; measure in that basis, XOR the $n/2$ answers classically. Lower bound $Q(\mathrm{PARITY}_n) \ge n/2$ even with bounded error, by the polynomial method below. $D = R = n$ (every bit matters on every input). Quantum advantage: a factor 2, no more.
- **Deutsch-Jozsa.** $f : \{0,1\}^m \to \{0,1\}$ promised constant or balanced, $n = 2^m$ input bits (the truth table). One phase query: $H^{\otimes m} O_f H^{\otimes m} |0^m\rangle$ has amplitude $\frac1n \sum_x (-1)^{f(x)} \in \{\pm 1, 0\}$ on $|0^m\rangle$: $Q_E = 1$. Deterministically $D = n/2 + 1$ (after $n/2$ equal values both cases are still possible). But $R = O(1)$: sample a few random $x$, a balanced $f$ shows both values with probability $\ge 1 - 2^{-(k-1)}$. So the separation is exact-vs-deterministic only.
- **Simon.** Promise $f(x) = f(y) \iff y \in \{x, x \oplus s\}$; find $s$. $Q = O(m)$ (each query yields a random $y \perp s$, $O(m)$ of them determine $s$ by linear algebra), $R = \Omega(2^{m/2})$ (birthday bound, an information-theoretic collision argument). Exponential separation, and the template for Shor's period finding. **Caveat:** it is a **partial** (promise) function. For total functions no exponential separation is possible: $D(f) = O(Q(f)^4)$ (Beals et al. proved $O(Q^6)$; Aaronson-Ben-David-Kothari-Tal 2020 improved to $O(Q^4)$ using Huang's sensitivity theorem), and it is tight: Ambainis, Balodis, Belovs, Lee, Santha, Smotrovs (2016) constructed a total $f$ with $D(f) = \tilde\Omega(Q(f)^4)$.

**Theorem (polynomial method; Beals, Buhrman, Cleve, Mosca, de Wolf).** After $t$ queries to the standard oracle, every amplitude of the state is a multilinear polynomial in $x_1, \dots, x_n$ of degree $\le t$; hence the acceptance probability $P(x)$ is a multilinear polynomial of degree $\le 2t$. Consequently $Q_E(f) \ge \deg(f)/2$ and $Q(f) \ge \widetilde{\deg}(f)/2$.
*Proof.* Induction on $t$. Write the state as $\sum_{i, b, w} \alpha_{i,b,w}(x) |i, b, w\rangle$ (index, oracle bit, workspace). $U_0|0\rangle$ has constant amplitudes (degree 0). The oracle maps $\alpha_{i,b,w} \mapsto (1 - x_i)\alpha_{i,b,w} + x_i \alpha_{i, 1-b, w}$: degree increases by at most 1, and since $x_i^2 = x_i$ on $\{0,1\}$ the result is still multilinear. A unitary $U_j$ forms linear combinations: degree unchanged. So amplitudes have degree $\le t$; $P(x) = \sum_{\text{accepting } (i,b,w)} |\alpha_{i,b,w}(x)|^2$ is a sum of products of two degree-$\le t$ polynomials (and their conjugates), degree $\le 2t$ after multilinear reduction. If the algorithm computes $f$ exactly, $P = f$ on $\{0,1\}^n$, so $2t \ge \deg(f)$; with error $1/3$, $|P - f| \le 1/3$, so $2t \ge \widetilde{\deg}(f)$. $\square$
With the phase oracle the same holds with $z_i = (-1)^{x_i} = 1 - 2x_i$ as variables; a subtlety visible in `query_complexity.py`: if the phase oracle acts on the whole index register (no "do not query" branch), every amplitude monomial has degree $\equiv t \pmod 2$ and $P$ has only even degrees; a dummy index with $x = 0$ (equivalently a controlled query) restores generality.

**Approximate degrees.**
- $\widetilde{\deg}(\mathrm{PARITY}_n) = \deg(\mathrm{PARITY}_n) = n$: **symmetrise** $p$ to $q(k) = \mathbb E_{|x| = k}\, p(x)$, a univariate polynomial of degree $\le \deg p$ (Minsky-Papert). $q(k) - 1/2$ alternates in sign at $k = 0, 1, \dots, n$ (it is $\le -1/6$ for even $k$ and $\ge 1/6$ for odd $k$), so $q - 1/2$ has $\ge n$ roots: degree $\ge n$. Hence $Q(\mathrm{PARITY}_n) \ge n/2$, matching Deutsch on pairs.
- $\widetilde{\deg}(\mathrm{OR}_n) = \Theta(\sqrt n)$ (Nisan-Szegedy). Lower bound: symmetrise to $q$ with $q(0) \in [-1/3, 1/3]$, $q(k) \in [2/3, 4/3]$ for $1 \le k \le n$. If $|q| \le c$ on the whole interval $[0, n]$, Markov's inequality $\max_{[0,n]} |q'| \le \frac{2 \deg(q)^2}{n} \max_{[0,n]} |q|$ together with $q(1) - q(0) \ge 1/3$ (so $|q'| \ge 1/3$ somewhere by the mean value theorem) gives $\deg q \ge \sqrt{n/(6c)}$. The boundedness of $q$ between the integers needs one more step (if $q$ is large somewhere, its derivative is large too, and Bernstein/Markov again yields the bound); the clean statement is $\deg q \ge \Omega(\sqrt n)$. Upper bound: the Chebyshev polynomial $T_d$ with $d = \Theta(\sqrt n)$ scaled to $[0, n]$ approximates OR. So $Q(\mathrm{OR}_n) = \Omega(\sqrt n)$ also follows from the polynomial method, matching Grover.
- General: $\widetilde{\deg}(f) \le 2 Q(f)$ always; for **symmetric** $f$ (depending only on $|x|$), $Q(f) = \Theta\big(\sqrt{n\,(n - \Gamma(f))}\big)$ where $\Gamma(f) = \min\{|2k - n + 1| : f_k \ne f_{k+1}\}$ measures how close to the middle the value changes (Beals et al.). OR: $\Gamma = n - 1$, $Q = \Theta(\sqrt n)$; PARITY, MAJORITY: $\Gamma \in \{0, 1\}$, $Q = \Theta(n)$. Total functions never gain more than a polynomial.

**Adversary method (Ambainis 2002 [S57], statement).** Let $X \subseteq f^{-1}(0)$, $Y \subseteq f^{-1}(1)$ and $R \subseteq X \times Y$ a relation such that every $x \in X$ has $\ge m$ partners, every $y \in Y$ has $\ge m'$ partners, and for every $i$ every $x$ has $\le \ell$ partners $y$ with $x_i \ne y_i$ and every $y$ has $\le \ell'$ such partners $x$. Then $Q(f) = \Omega\big(\sqrt{m m' / (\ell \ell')}\big)$. For OR: $X = \{0^n\}$, $Y = \{e_i\}$, $R = X \times Y$: $m = n$, $m' = \ell = \ell' = 1$, so $\Omega(\sqrt n)$ in one line; the proof is the BBBV progress-measure argument generalised: a weighted sum of $|\langle \psi_x | \psi_y \rangle|$ over $R$ must drop from $|R|$ to a constant fraction and each query decreases it by at most $\sqrt{\ell \ell' / (m m')}\,|R|$. The **general (negative-weight) adversary bound** characterises $Q(f)$ up to constants for every function (Høyer-Lee-Špalek, Reichardt 2009); the polynomial method can be weaker (e.g. for the collision problem it needed a separate argument, Aaronson-Shi) or stronger in other cases.

## Worked example

*Deutsch's parity as a polynomial.* Phase query on $\frac{1}{\sqrt 2}(|0\rangle + |1\rangle)$, then measure $|-\rangle$: $P(x) = \big|\tfrac12 (z_0 - z_1)\big|^2 = \tfrac12(1 - z_0 z_1)$ with $z_i = 1 - 2x_i$, i.e. $P = x_0 + x_1 - 2 x_0 x_1$: degree $2 = 2t$, the multilinear form of XOR. `multilinear_coefficients` returns $[0, 1, 1, -2]$ for the monomials $1, x_0, x_1, x_0 x_1$.

*Grover counts.* $n = 16$: $\theta = \arcsin(1/4) = 0.2527$, $\frac{\pi}{4}\sqrt{16} = 3.14$, take $r = 3$: success $\sin^2(7 \theta) = 0.961$. $n = 256$: $r = 12$, success $0.9999$. Classical: 8 resp. 128 expected queries. The BBBV bound says no algorithm beats $c\sqrt n$; Zalka showed Grover's constant $\pi/4$ is optimal.

*Feynman sum by hand.* $\langle 0 | H Z H | 0\rangle = \sum_{y \in \{0,1\}} \langle 0|H|y\rangle \langle y|Z|y\rangle \langle y|H|0\rangle = \tfrac12 (+1) + \tfrac12 (-1) = 0$: two paths cancel. In general $m$ qubits and $\ell$ gates give $2^{m(\ell-1)}$ paths but a path is $m\ell$ bits: the PSPACE simulation stores one path and one running sum, never the $2^m$-dimensional state vector. The classical simulation cost is exponential in *time* (paths) or in *space* (state vector), never polynomial in both, as far as anyone knows: that is the content of "BQP is probably not in BPP".

*Symmetric function bound.* MAJORITY$_n$ ($n$ odd): $f_k$ changes at $k = (n-1)/2$, $\Gamma = |2 \cdot \tfrac{n-1}{2} - n + 1| = 0$, so $Q = \Theta(n)$: no quantum speedup for majority beyond constants, versus $\Theta(\sqrt n)$ for OR whose change is at $k = 0$.

*Solovay-Kitaev overhead.* A circuit of $\ell = 10^6$ gates from one universal set compiled into Clifford+T with total error $0.01$: per-gate $\varepsilon = 10^{-8}$; with $c = 3.97$, $\log^{3.97}(10^8) \approx 18.4^{3.97} \approx 10^{5}$ gates per original gate, $10^{11}$ total; with Ross-Selinger synthesis about $3 \log_2(1/\varepsilon) \approx 80$ T-gates per single-qubit rotation, $10^{8}$ total. Polynomial either way.

## Pitfalls

- BQP is the analogue of BPP, defined with bounded error and **uniform** circuits. "There exists a polynomial-size quantum circuit" without uniformity is BQP/poly, which contains undecidable unary languages (B04).
- Shor's algorithm does **not** show $\mathrm{NP} \subseteq \mathrm{BQP}$: FACTORING is not known to be NP-hard and is in $\mathrm{NP} \cap \mathrm{coNP}$ (B01). Grover does not put NP in BQP either: $2^{n/2}$ is still exponential.
- $\mathrm{BQP} \subseteq \mathrm{PP}$ is the best classical upper bound; $\mathrm{BQP} \subseteq \mathrm{PH}$ is unknown and there is an oracle against it (Raz-Tal).
- Oracle (relativised) separations are evidence, not proof: $\mathrm{NP}^A \not\subseteq \mathrm{BQP}^A$ for some $A$ is compatible with $\mathrm{NP} \subseteq \mathrm{BQP}$ in the unrelativised world (Baker-Gill-Solovay gives oracles on both sides of P vs NP).
- Deutsch-Jozsa separates $Q_E$ from $D$, not $Q$ from $R$; a randomised classical algorithm needs $O(1)$ queries. Only promise problems (Simon, period finding) give exponential $Q$ vs $R$ separations; for total functions $D \le O(Q^4)$.
- The QMA witness is a quantum state: the verifier cannot copy it (no-cloning), so amplification needs Marriott-Watrous or a product-witness argument; the verifier also cannot "read" it, only measure it once.
- $k$-LH needs the promise gap $b - a \ge 1/\mathrm{poly}$; with an exponentially small gap the problem becomes PSPACE-hard (Fefferman-Lin). And $k$-LH with $k = 1$ is in P (diagonalise each term).
- In the polynomial method the degree bound is on the **acceptance probability** ($2t$), not on the amplitudes ($t$); forgetting the factor 2 halves every lower bound. Also, a low-degree approximating polynomial does not imply a fast algorithm; the method gives lower bounds only.
- Parity on $n$ bits: the quantum speedup is exactly a factor 2, $\lceil n/2 \rceil$ queries; it is a common exam trap to claim $O(1)$ or $O(\sqrt n)$.

## Exam-style questions

1. *Define BQP and explain why the choice of $2/3$ and of the gate set does not matter.* Uniform polynomial-size circuits with error $\le 1/3$; Chernoff majority amplification; Solovay-Kitaev with $\mathrm{polylog}(1/\varepsilon)$ overhead and additive error accumulation.
2. *Prove $\mathrm{BPP} \subseteq \mathrm{BQP}$.* Boolean circuit to reversible circuit (Toffoli for NAND, CNOT for fan-out), uncompute garbage, coins from $H|0\rangle$.
3. *Prove $\mathrm{BQP} \subseteq \mathrm{PSPACE}$ and indicate why the same argument gives $\mathrm{BQP} \subseteq \mathrm{PP}$.* Feynman path sum, enumerate paths in polynomial space; with $\{H, \mathrm{Toffoli}\}$ each path contributes $\pm 2^{-h/2}$, so amplitudes are differences of #P counts.
4. *Define QMA and $k$-LOCAL HAMILTONIAN; sketch why $k$-LH $\in$ QMA and what the history state is.* Ground state as witness, measure a random term, acceptance $1 - \langle H \rangle / r$; history state $\frac{1}{\sqrt{T+1}}\sum_t U_t \cdots U_1|\xi,0\rangle|t\rangle$ is the zero-energy state of $H_{\mathrm{prop}}$.
5. *State the polynomial method and use it to show $Q(\mathrm{PARITY}_n) \ge n/2$.* Acceptance probability is a degree-$\le 2t$ multilinear polynomial approximating $f$; symmetrisation gives a univariate polynomial with $n$ sign changes, degree $\ge n$.
6. *Why does Grover's algorithm not imply $\mathrm{NP} \subseteq \mathrm{BQP}$, and what does BBBV say?* $\sqrt{2^n}$ is exponential; BBBV: any quantum algorithm for unstructured search needs $\Omega(\sqrt N)$ queries, so relative to an oracle NP is not in BQP.
7. *Give $Q$, $R$, $D$ for OR$_n$, PARITY$_n$, Deutsch-Jozsa and Simon, and say which separations survive for total functions.* $(\sqrt n, n, n)$, $(n/2, n, n)$, $(1, O(1), n/2 + 1)$, $(O(m), 2^{\Omega(m)}, 2^{\Omega(m)})$; for total functions at most polynomial: $D = O(Q^4)$.
