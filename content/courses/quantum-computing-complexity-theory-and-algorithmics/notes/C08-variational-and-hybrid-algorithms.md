# C08 Variational and hybrid quantum-classical algorithms

Shor and Grover need fault-tolerant machines. On today's noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) hardware the only executable algorithms are *hybrid*: a short parametrised quantum circuit prepares a state, measurements estimate an expectation value, and a classical optimiser updates the parameters. This note covers the two canonical examples, the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) for ground-state energies and the quantum approximate optimisation algorithm (QAOA) for MaxCut, the parameter-shift gradient rule, and the barren-plateau obstruction. References: primary - Peruzzo et al. [S49], Farhi-Goldstone-Gutmann [S48], McClean et al. [S51], Cerezo et al. [S52], O'Malley et al. for the H2 coefficients [S50], Goemans-Williamson [S57]; Nielsen & Chuang 4.7 for the simulation background [S30]; Aaronson for the sceptical view [S27]. **This topic is not in the sibling quantum courses** [S15, S20]: it appears on the 192.043 subject list [S1] and its treatment lives in 194.027 Hybrid Quantum-Classical Systems, whose lecturer De Maio was a 192.043 lecturer in 2024W [S3, S10, S22]. Exam 2 material.

## Definitions

**Variational principle.** For a Hermitian $H$ with ground energy $E_0$ and any normalised $|\psi\rangle$: $\langle\psi|H|\psi\rangle\geq E_0$, with equality iff $|\psi\rangle$ is a ground state. Hence $E_0 = \min_\theta\langle\psi(\theta)|H|\psi(\theta)\rangle$ over any family containing the ground state, and an upper bound otherwise.

**Parametrised circuit (ansatz).** $|\psi(\theta)\rangle = U(\theta)|0^n\rangle$ with $U(\theta) = \prod_l U_l(\theta_l)$, typically $U_l = e^{-i\theta_lP_l/2}$ for Pauli strings $P_l$ interleaved with fixed entangling gates. *Hardware-efficient ansatz:* layers of $R_y$ (or $R_y,R_z$) on every qubit followed by a ladder of CNOTs. *Problem-inspired ansatz:* unitary coupled cluster (UCC) in chemistry, QAOA layers in optimisation.

**Pauli decomposition.** Any $n$-qubit Hermitian $H = \sum_j c_jP_j$ with real $c_j$ and Pauli strings $P_j\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}$ ($c_j = 2^{-n}\mathrm{tr}(P_jH)$). $\langle H\rangle = \sum_jc_j\langle P_j\rangle$, and each $\langle P_j\rangle$ is estimated by measuring in the eigenbasis of $P_j$ (rotate $X\to Z$ by $H$, $Y\to Z$ by $S^\dagger H$, then read $\pm1$ from the parities). Commuting Pauli strings that are *qubit-wise* commuting can share one measurement setting.

**VQE.** Minimise $E(\theta) = \langle\psi(\theta)|H|\psi(\theta)\rangle$ with a classical optimiser; the quantum computer only evaluates $E$ (and possibly gradients).

**MaxCut.** Graph $G=(V,E)$; find $S\subseteq V$ maximising the number of edges between $S$ and $V\setminus S$. NP-hard; Goemans–Williamson achieves ratio $0.878$ classically [S57] (A07). Encoding: $z_i = \pm1$ for vertex $i$; cut value $C(z) = \sum_{(i,j)\in E}\frac{1-z_iz_j}{2}$; the cost Hamiltonian $C = \sum_{(i,j)\in E}\frac{1}{2}(I - Z_iZ_j)$ is diagonal with $C|z\rangle = C(z)|z\rangle$.

**QAOA with $p$ layers.** Mixer $B = \sum_iX_i$. State
$$|\gamma,\beta\rangle = e^{-i\beta_pB}e^{-i\gamma_pC}\cdots e^{-i\beta_1B}e^{-i\gamma_1C}|+\rangle^{\otimes n},$$
maximise $F_p(\gamma,\beta) = \langle\gamma,\beta|C|\gamma,\beta\rangle$; sample $z$ from the optimised state. Each $e^{-i\gamma C}$ is a product of $e^{i\gamma Z_iZ_j/2}$ (a CNOT–$R_z(-\gamma)$–CNOT per edge) and $e^{-i\beta B} = \prod_iR_x(2\beta)$.

## Results

**Shot noise.** Estimating $\langle P\rangle$ from $M$ shots has standard error $\sqrt{(1-\langle P\rangle^2)/M}\leq M^{-1/2}$; for $\langle H\rangle$ to precision $\epsilon$ one needs $M = O\big((\sum_j|c_j|)^2/\epsilon^2\big)$ shots. Chemical accuracy $\epsilon\approx1.6$ mHa on molecules with $10^4$–$10^6$ terms is the practical bottleneck.

**Parameter-shift rule** [S52]**.** For a gate $U(\theta)=e^{-i\theta P/2}$ with $P^2=I$ (any Pauli string or single-qubit rotation), and $E(\theta) = \langle 0|V^\dagger U(\theta)^\dagger W^\dagger H W U(\theta)V|0\rangle$ for fixed unitaries $V$ (before) and $W$ (after):
$$\frac{\partial E}{\partial\theta} = \frac{1}{2}\Big[E\big(\theta+\tfrac\pi2\big)-E\big(\theta-\tfrac\pi2\big)\Big].$$
*Derivation.* $U(\theta)=\cos\frac\theta2\,I - i\sin\frac\theta2\,P$. Let $|\phi\rangle = V|0\rangle$ and $\tilde H = W^\dagger HW$. Then $E(\theta) = \cos^2\frac\theta2\langle\tilde H\rangle_\phi + \sin^2\frac\theta2\langle P\tilde HP\rangle_\phi + \sin\frac\theta2\cos\frac\theta2\,\langle i[P,\tilde H]\rangle_\phi$, i.e. $E(\theta) = A + B\cos\theta + C\sin\theta$ for $\theta$-independent $A,B,C$: a single Fourier mode. For such a function $E'(\theta) = -B\sin\theta+C\cos\theta$ and $\frac12[E(\theta+\frac\pi2)-E(\theta-\frac\pi2)] = \frac12[B(\cos(\theta+\frac\pi2)-\cos(\theta-\frac\pi2)) + C(\sin(\theta+\frac\pi2)-\sin(\theta-\frac\pi2))] = -B\sin\theta+C\cos\theta$. $\square$ Unlike finite differences, the rule is exact and uses the same shot budget at two well-separated points, so it is robust to shot noise. Gradient of an $L$-parameter circuit: $2L$ circuit evaluations.

**Optimisers.** Gradient-free (COBYLA, Nelder–Mead, SPSA which needs only 2 evaluations per step regardless of $L$) or gradient-based (BFGS, Adam with parameter-shift gradients). The landscape is non-convex; multiple restarts and problem-informed initialisation (e.g. Hartree–Fock for chemistry, adiabatic-like linear ramps for QAOA) matter more than the optimiser.

**QAOA facts.** (i) $F_p$ is nondecreasing in $p$ and $\lim_{p\to\infty}\max F_p = \max C$ (Trotterised adiabatic evolution from the ground state of $-B$, which is $|+\rangle^{\otimes n}$, to the ground state of $-C$). (ii) For $p=1$ on a single edge, $F_1 = \frac12\big(1+\sin4\beta\sin\gamma\big)$ (derivation below), maximised to $1$ at $\beta = \pi/8$, $\gamma=\pi/2$. (iii) For 3-regular graphs, $p=1$ guarantees ratio $\geq0.6924$ (Farhi et al. [S48]); for the ring $C_n$ with $p$ layers the ratio is $\frac{2p+1}{2p+2}$ ($0.75$ at $p=1$). (iv) Because $e^{-i\gamma C}$ has locality $p$ ("light cone"), $F_p$ on a bounded-degree graph is a sum of terms each depending on the subgraph within distance $p$ of an edge; for fixed $p$ it can be computed classically in polynomial time, so QAOA's potential advantage lies in sampling, not in evaluating $F_p$. (v) $p=1$ QAOA sampling is already hard to simulate classically under plausible complexity assumptions (Farhi–Harrow), but that is not an *optimisation* advantage.

*Derivation of (ii).* Heisenberg picture on $|{+}{+}\rangle$: $e^{i\beta X}Ze^{-i\beta X} = Z\cos2\beta+Y\sin2\beta$, so $Z_0Z_1\mapsto(cZ_0+sY_0)(cZ_1+sY_1)$ with $c=\cos2\beta$, $s=\sin2\beta$. Conjugating by $e^{-i\gamma Z_0Z_1/2}$ leaves $Z_0Z_1$ and $Y_0Y_1$ invariant and sends $Z_0Y_1\mapsto Z_0Y_1\cos\gamma - X_1\sin\gamma$, $Y_0Z_1\mapsto Y_0Z_1\cos\gamma-X_0\sin\gamma$. In $|{+}{+}\rangle$ only $\langle X_i\rangle = 1$ survives: $\langle Z_0Z_1\rangle = -2cs\sin\gamma = -\sin4\beta\sin\gamma$, hence $F_1 = \frac12(1+\sin4\beta\sin\gamma)$. $\square$

**Barren plateaus (McClean et al. 2018 [S51]).** For an ansatz whose distribution over unitaries (at random parameters) forms a unitary 2-design on $n$ qubits (already true for hardware-efficient circuits of depth $O(n)$ or of polynomial depth), for any local or global observable $H$ with $\|H\|=O(1)$:
$$\mathbb{E}_\theta\Big[\frac{\partial E}{\partial\theta_k}\Big]=0,\qquad \mathrm{Var}_\theta\Big[\frac{\partial E}{\partial\theta_k}\Big]\;\leq\;\frac{c}{2^n}\quad\text{(indeed }\Theta(2^{-n})\text{)}.$$
*Idea.* Write $U = U_-U_+$ with the differentiated gate between. Haar-averaging $U_+$ (or $U_-$) with the 2-design formulas $\int dU\,U\rho U^\dagger = \mathrm{tr}(\rho)\frac{I}{2^n}$ and $\int dU\,\mathrm{tr}(U A U^\dagger B\,UCU^\dagger D)$-type integrals (Weingarten calculus) gives a variance proportional to $\mathrm{tr}(H^2)\mathrm{tr}(\rho^2)/(4^n-1)\sim 2^{-n}$. By Chebyshev, the gradient is exponentially small except with exponentially small probability, so gradient descent (and any method estimating $E$ to polynomial precision with polynomially many shots) is stuck at a random start. Extensions: local cost functions and shallow circuits ($\log n$ depth) avoid it (Cerezo et al. 2021); noise induces plateaus regardless of the ansatz; problem-inspired ansätze and warm starts are the practical mitigation. Recent "no-go" results (2023–24) note that whenever a plateau is provably absent, the circuit is often classically simulable — a warning sign for VQE-style advantage claims.

**Hybrid landscape and critical perspective.** Other hybrids: quantum annealing (D-Wave: analogue adiabatic optimisation, not gate-based, no proven speedup), variational quantum linear solvers, quantum machine learning with parametrised circuits, classical shadows for efficient estimation of many observables from few measurements. No variational algorithm has a proven speedup; VQE's chemistry advantage is limited by shot counts, QAOA at low $p$ is often beaten by simple classical heuristics, and "dequantisation" results (Tang 2019 for recommendation systems) removed some claimed exponential speedups in quantum ML. The honest summary: hybrids are the only algorithms runnable today, useful as hardware benchmarks and possibly for specific chemistry instances; the proven speedups (Shor, Grover, phase estimation for chemistry) need error correction.

## Worked example

**VQE for H$_2$ (STO-3G, 0.735 Å, two-qubit reduction).**
$$H = -1.0523\,II + 0.3979\,ZI - 0.3979\,IZ - 0.0113\,ZZ + 0.1809\,XX\quad(\text{Hartree}).$$
$H$ commutes with $Z_0Z_1$ (parity), so it is block-diagonal in the even sector $\{|00\rangle,|11\rangle\}$ and the odd sector $\{|01\rangle,|10\rangle\}$. Diagonal entries: $\langle z|H|z\rangle = -1.0523 + 0.3979(z_0 - z_1) - 0.0113\,z_0z_1$ with $z_i=\pm1$ (eigenvalue of $Z$ on $|0\rangle,|1\rangle$):
$|00\rangle: -1.0636$, $|11\rangle: -1.0636$, $|01\rangle: -1.0523+0.7958+0.0113 = -0.2452$, $|10\rangle: -1.0523-0.7958+0.0113=-1.8368$. $XX$ couples $|00\rangle\leftrightarrow|11\rangle$ and $|01\rangle\leftrightarrow|10\rangle$ with $0.1809$.
Odd block: $\begin{pmatrix}-0.2452&0.1809\\0.1809&-1.8368\end{pmatrix}$, eigenvalues $-1.0410\pm\sqrt{0.7958^2+0.1809^2} = -1.0410\pm0.8161$, so $E_0 = -1.8571$ Ha and the other odd level is $-0.2249$. The published value at $0.735$ Å in STO-3G is $-1.8572$ Ha [S50]; the $10^{-4}$ gap is the rounding of the five coefficients. Even block: $-1.0636\pm0.1809 = -1.2445, -0.8827$. The ground state is $\cos\frac{\alpha}{2}|10\rangle - \sin\frac\alpha2|01\rangle$ with $\tan\alpha = 0.1809/0.7958$, $\alpha = 0.2235$: the Hartree–Fock state $|10\rangle$ with a small admixture of the doubly excited $|01\rangle$.
Ansatz that reaches it exactly: $|10\rangle\to R_y(\theta)$ on qubit 0 followed by CNOT$_{0\to1}$ gives $\cos\frac\theta2|10\rangle+\sin\frac\theta2|01\rangle$ (one parameter, UCC-like); $E(\theta) = -1.0410 - 0.7958\cos\theta + 0.1809\sin\theta$, minimised at $\tan\theta = -0.1809/0.7958$, $E = -1.8571$. A two-layer hardware-efficient $R_y$–CNOT ansatz from $|00\rangle$ also reaches $10^{-3}$ Ha in `vqe.py`. Measurement settings: $\{ZI, IZ, ZZ\}$ from one $Z$-basis setting and $XX$ from one $X$-basis setting: 2 settings per energy evaluation.

**QAOA $p=1$ on the ring $C_4$** (4 vertices, max cut 4): by the light-cone argument each edge's contribution equals the single-edge formula only when neighbours do not interfere; for $C_4$ the exact optimum of $F_1$ is $3$ (ratio $3/4 = \frac{2p+1}{2p+2}$). Sampling the optimised state gives the two perfect cuts $0101, 1010$ as the most likely bitstrings; `qaoa.py` verifies this and reaches $\geq0.85\cdot\max C$ at $p=2$.

## Pitfalls

- VQE returns an upper bound on $E_0$; a low energy from a bad ansatz is not "closer to the truth" than a higher one from a converged calculation unless the variance $\langle H^2\rangle-\langle H\rangle^2$ is also small.
- Parameter shift needs generators with eigenvalues $\pm\frac12$ (i.e. $e^{-i\theta P/2}$ with $P^2=I$); for other gates use the general (stochastic) shift rules or decompose.
- Using finite differences with shot noise: error $\sim\sqrt{1/M}/h$, blows up as $h\to0$.
- QAOA's $\gamma$ is periodic with period $2\pi$ for integer-weighted $C$, and $\beta$ with period $\pi$; with symmetry, restrict to $\gamma\in[0,\pi)$, $\beta\in[0,\pi/2)$ to avoid redundant optimisation.
- Claiming "QAOA is a quantum speedup for MaxCut": no such result exists; at fixed $p$ the value $F_p$ is classically computable, and Goemans–Williamson beats low-$p$ QAOA's worst-case guarantee.
- Barren plateaus are a property of the *ansatz family and cost function*, not of the optimiser; switching optimisers does not help.

## Exam-style questions

1. *State and prove the parameter-shift rule for $U(\theta)=e^{-i\theta Z/2}$.* $E(\theta) = A+B\cos\theta+C\sin\theta$ (shown above), so $E'(\theta) = \frac12[E(\theta+\pi/2)-E(\theta-\pi/2)]$.
2. *Compute $F_1(\gamma,\beta)$ for a single edge and its maximum.* $\frac12(1+\sin4\beta\sin\gamma)$; maximum $1$ at $\beta=\pi/8,\gamma=\pi/2$: one layer cuts one edge perfectly.
3. *Explain why $|+\rangle^{\otimes n}$ is the QAOA initial state and what $p\to\infty$ corresponds to.* It is the ground state of $-B$; the QAOA sequence Trotterises the adiabatic path $H(s) = -(1-s)B - sC$ from $-B$ to $-C$, whose final ground state is the optimal cut; the adiabatic theorem guarantees success for slow enough evolution, i.e. large $p$.
4. *A 20-qubit hardware-efficient VQE with random initialisation shows gradients of size $10^{-3}$ with standard error $10^{-2}$ per shot batch. Diagnose.* Barren plateau: gradient variance $\Theta(2^{-n})\approx10^{-6}$, so typical gradients $\sim10^{-3}$, below the shot noise; increasing shots by $\sim10^4$ would be needed. Remedies: problem-inspired ansatz, local cost, shallow circuit, warm start (e.g. Hartree–Fock).
5. *For the two-qubit H$_2$ Hamiltonian, show the ground state has odd parity and derive its energy.* $[H,Z_0Z_1]=0$; the odd block $\{|01\rangle,|10\rangle\}$ has diagonal $-0.2452,-1.8368$ and coupling $0.1809$, giving $-1.8571$, lower than the even block's $-1.2445$.
6. *Why is estimating $F_p$ classically easy for fixed $p$ on bounded-degree graphs, and why does this not make QAOA pointless?* Each edge term depends only on the radius-$p$ neighbourhood (light cone), a bounded-size subgraph whose contribution is a small computation. Sampling low-energy strings from the QAOA state is what a classical simulation cannot obviously do; the optimisation of $(\gamma,\beta)$ itself can be done classically.
