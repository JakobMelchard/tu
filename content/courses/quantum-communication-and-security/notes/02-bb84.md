# 02 The BB84 protocol

Second TISS topic [S2]; learning outcome "describe the steps of a QKD protocol". BB84 [S6] encodes bits in two mutually unbiased qubit bases; Eve cannot learn a bit without knowing the basis, and guessing the basis wrong causes errors that Alice and Bob measure. This note does the protocol, the intercept-resend attack, the QBER and its estimation; the proof that the QBER bounds *every* attack is note 05.

## Definitions

1. **States.** $Z$ basis $\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}$, $X$ basis $\{|+\rangle,|-\rangle\}$, $|\pm\rangle=(|0\rangle\pm|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$. Mutually unbiased: $|\langle a|b\rangle|^2=\tfrac12$ across bases, so the overlap constant is $c=\max|\langle a|b\rangle|^2=\tfrac12$ [S5 Eq. (21)].
2. **Protocol (prepare-and-measure)** [S6, S3 §II.B].
   1. *Preparation.* For $i=1..N$ Alice draws a bit $x_i$ and a basis $\theta_i\in\{Z,X\}$ uniformly and sends $|x_i\rangle_{\theta_i}$.
   2. *Measurement.* Bob measures in a uniformly random basis $\theta'_i$, outcome $y_i$.
   3. *Sifting.* Over the authenticated channel they announce $\theta_i,\theta'_i$ (never $x_i,y_i$) and keep rounds with $\theta_i=\theta'_i$: the **sifted key**, $\approx N/2$ bits.
   4. *Parameter estimation.* They reveal a random subset of $k$ sifted positions, compute the **QBER** $\hat e=\frac1k\sum\mathbb 1[x_i\ne y_i]$ and abort if $\hat e>\delta$.
   5. *Information reconciliation.* Alice sends $\mathrm{leak_{EC}}$ bits of syndrome, Bob corrects, a $t$-bit hash verifies (note 04).
   6. *Privacy amplification.* Both apply a random two-universal hash to $\ell$ bits (note 04).
3. **QBER.** Fraction of sifted positions with $x_i\ne y_i$; per basis $e_Z,e_X$.
4. **Entanglement-based version (BBM92)** [S37]. A source emits $|\Phi^+\rangle=(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2$; Alice and Bob each measure $Z$ or $X$ at random. Because $\langle\Phi^+|(P\otimes P')|\Phi^+\rangle$ reproduces the prepare-and-measure statistics, Alice measuring her half *is* preparing a random BB84 state (source replacement). Security proofs work in this picture (note 05).
5. **Intercept-resend attack.** Eve measures a fraction $p$ of the qubits in a basis of her choice and re-sends the eigenstate she found.
6. **Depolarising channel.** $\rho\mapsto(1-p)\rho+p\,\mathbb 1/2$.

## Results

**Proposition 2.1 (intercept-resend QBER)** [S3 §I.B]. Eve on every qubit, random basis: $e=\tfrac14$ and $I(A{:}E)=\tfrac12$ per sifted bit. On a fraction $p$: $e=p/4$, $I(A{:}E)=p/2=2e$.

*Derivation.* Condition on a sifted round (Alice and Bob in the same basis). With probability $\tfrac12$ Eve chose the same basis: she learns $x$ and re-sends the correct state, no error. With probability $\tfrac12$ she chose the other basis: her outcome is uniform, the re-sent eigenstate is unbiased in Alice's basis, Bob's result is uniform, error $\tfrac12$. So $e=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12=\tfrac14$. She knows $x$ exactly in the first case and nothing in the second: $I(A{:}E)=\tfrac12$. Linearity in $p$ gives the rest. ∎

**Corollary 2.2 (Csiszár-Körner rate for this attack).** For one-way post-processing against an Eve with classical information, the achievable rate is $I(A{:}B)-I(A{:}E)$ [S3 §I.B; §III.B Eq. (21)]. With $I(A{:}B)=1-h(e)$ (binary symmetric channel) the intercept-resend attack allows $r=1-h(e)-2e>0$ up to $e=17.05\%$. This is **not** the security threshold: optimal coherent attacks give $11.00\%$ (note 05).

**Proposition 2.3 (biased Eve).** If Eve always measures $Z$: $e_Z=0$, $e_X=\tfrac12$ (per basis, full attack), total $\tfrac14$. Basis-resolved QBERs detect the bias; the finite-key proof bounds Eve via the **$X$-basis** error when the key is from $Z$ [S5 §6.2-6.3].

**Proposition 2.4 (noise and attack combined).** Depolarising noise $p$ after Eve's fraction $p_{ir}$:
$$e=(1-p)\,\frac{p_{ir}}4+\frac p2 .$$
Only the value of $e$ matters for the key rate: Alice and Bob must attribute *all* errors to Eve.

**Proposition 2.5 (sampling bound)** [S5 Lemma 6, from Serfling [S38]]. $n$ key bits and $k$ test bits drawn uniformly without replacement from a fixed string:
$$\Pr\bigl[\text{error rate on key}\ge\hat e+\nu\bigr]\le\exp\Bigl(-\frac{2\nu^2nk^2}{(n+k)(k+1)}\Bigr)\approx e^{-2\nu^2k}\ (n\gg k).$$
No i.i.d. assumption: the randomness is the choice of test positions, which Eve does not know.

**Sifting efficiency.** Uniform bases keep $\tfrac12$. Choosing $Z$ with probability $p_Z\to1$ keeps $p_Z^2+p_X^2\to1$ ("efficient BB84", sift factor $q\approx1$ [S9, text at Eq. (1)]); the test basis then needs enough rounds for estimation.

| 0.4 | 0 | 0.499 | 0.0989 | 0.10 | 0.2001 | 0.2 |
| 0 | 0.1 | 0.498 | 0.0500 | 0.05 | 0 | 0 |
| 0.4 | 0.1 | 0.501 | 0.1392 | 0.14 | 0.1983 | 0.2 |

**Parameter estimation budget.** Sifted key $10^6$, true QBER $2\%$. Test $k=10^4$ bits: $\sigma(\hat e)=\sqrt{0.02\cdot0.98/10^4}=0.0014$. The rigorous bound of Prop. 2.5 at $\nu=0.01$ is only $e^{-2}\approx0.14$; to reach $\varepsilon_{\rm pe}=10^{-10}$ at $\nu=0.01$ needs $2\nu^2k\approx23$, i.e. $k\approx1.2\times10^5$. This is why finite keys lose so much (note 05: $\ell/m=0.52$ at $m=10^5$ vs $0.83$ asymptotically for $\delta=1\%$).

**Key after post-processing (asymptotic).** $e=2\%$: $h(0.02)=0.1414$. Shor-Preskill $1-2h=0.717$; with realistic reconciliation $f=1.16$: $1-(1+f)h=0.694$, i.e. $6.9\times10^5$ secret bits from $10^6$ sifted bits. At $e=25\%$ (full intercept-resend) the protocol aborts.

## Pitfalls

- Bases are announced **after** Bob's measurement. Announcing before lets Eve measure in the right basis without error.
- The 25 % QBER is for a full intercept-resend in random bases; it is one attack, not a bound. The threshold for general attacks is 11 % (one-way, no preprocessing) [S7, S3 §III.B].
- Mixing bases in the QBER can hide a biased attack (Prop. 2.3). The proof in [S5] uses the error rate in the basis *conjugate* to the key basis.
- The test bits are discarded; revealing them is fine, reusing them is not.
- Sifting leaks bases, not bits: $I(\text{bases}:x)=0$. Error correction leaks bits (counted in $\mathrm{leak_{EC}}$).
- Prepare-and-measure ≠ entanglement-based for free: the equivalence needs Alice's source to be exactly the BB84 states (no side channels, basis-independent) [S5 §9, S15]. Multi-photon pulses break it (note 06).

## Questions

1. *List the BB84 steps and say what is public.* Preparation, measurement, sifting (bases public), parameter estimation (test bits public), error correction (syndrome and hash public), privacy amplification (hash seed public). Only the unrevealed sifted bits are secret.
2. *Derive the QBER of the full intercept-resend attack.* Same basis half the time (no error), wrong basis half the time (error $\tfrac12$): $e=\tfrac14$.
3. *Eve attacks a fraction $p$ of the qubits. Give QBER and her information. Is a key possible at $e=10\%$ against this attack?* $e=p/4$, $I(A{:}E)=2e$. At $e=0.1$: $1-h(0.1)-0.2=1-0.469-0.2=0.331>0$, yes; but against general attacks $1-2h(0.1)=0.062$ only.
4. *Why does an entanglement-based proof apply to prepare-and-measure BB84?* Source replacement: Alice measuring half of $|\Phi^+\rangle$ in $Z$ or $X$ prepares the BB84 ensemble on the other half with identical statistics; Eve's possibilities are the same.
5. *How many test bits for $\nu=0.02$, $\varepsilon_{\rm pe}=10^{-10}$?* $2\nu^2k\approx\ln(10^{10})=23$: $k\approx23/(2\cdot4\times10^{-4})\approx2.9\times10^4$.
